Overgrown

Tells you when and how much to water each of your plants, and when to protect them from the weather.

It works from three things: care data for the species, the plant’s actual situation (pot, place, light, local weather) and your own answers, from which it learns each plant. It runs entirely on the phone. No account, no server, no analytics, no advertising.

01A screen only as loud as your plants need

Needs water

How much, in millilitres, for this pot. Tap Watered on the card or in the notification.

A question

“How does the soil feel?” Wet, OK or Dry. Each answer teaches it how fast this plant really drinks.

Weather alert

One card per event, not per day: “Cold nights Mon–Wed. Move Boris against the wall.”

Colour means state, and never on its own: every state also has words and a shape. Plants that are fine stay quiet.

02How it decides

The pot is a bucket of water the roots can reach. Every day the weather, the plant and the pot draw on it; rain can refill it if it reaches the soil. When the bucket gets low for that kind of plant, it is time to water, and the amount is what was used. Change the plant below and watch the next ten days move.

A sketch of the idea with made-up weather, not the app’s engine. The real one uses the forecast’s evapotranspiration, the sun’s angle for indoor plants, pot height and shape, wind and rain exposure, and updates its belief about each plant from every answer with an interval likelihood rather than a fixed step.

03Weather, read for each pot

Forecasts come from Open-Meteo and MET Norway, fetched for the place, not the plant. Each plant then reads them through its own situation: a balcony under another balcony gets no rain, a pot against a south wall dries faster, a pot on the ground freezes before the plant does.

Frost, heat and storm warnings arrive as one card per event, naming every plant that needs moving, with what to do.

04Tested on ten years of real weather

Before any interface, the engine was run against simulated plants that behave in more ways than the engine models (hourly demand from the sun’s angle and air dryness, a random thirst, pot-wall loss, wind, rain at random hours) and a simulated person whose soil answers are noisy and biased. Weather is 2015–2024 daily history for each city. The baseline uses the same inputs and learning, but a fixed summer interval.

Days per plant per year the soil got drier than the plant can take. From docs/reports/sim-report.md. This shows robustness, not real-world accuracy: that needs kitchen-scale measurements, which come next.

05Your plants stay on your phone

  • No account. Nothing to sign up for.
  • No server. Nothing you enter is sent to the developer.
  • No tracking. No analytics, crash reporting or advertising.
  • Yours to take. Export everything, photos included, to one file.

The only things that leave the phone are weather requests and city searches, sent straight to the weather provider with each place rounded to about 1 km. No plant data, no identifiers.

06Source

Kotlin and Jetpack Compose. The engine is plain Kotlin Multiplatform with no Android code, so it can be tested on its own and run anywhere.

git clone https://github.com/stcksmsh/overgrown
cd overgrown
./gradlew build

Needs JDK 21 and the Android SDK (platform 36). The care database is a git submodule in its own repository, which is not public yet.
